# Literature review: Detect different lies

Reviewed 2026-10-04. Scope: one fixed model, matched factual questions, and whether detectors distinguish epistemic errors from incentive-associated misreporting. Ten PDFs and all sections of the supplied Anthropic article were read. Bibliography and local PDFs: [papers/README.md](papers/README.md); sequential all-chunk notes: [notes/reading_notes.md](notes/reading_notes.md). This is an actionable narrative review, not a systematic meta-analysis. Numerical results below are published claims, not replications conducted here.

## Research area and central distinction
Factuality concerns agreement with the world; honesty concerns agreement with the model’s available beliefs. Neither correct answers nor false answers establish honesty by themselves. A model can consistently believe something wrong, and a statement contradicting a wrong belief can accidentally be true. The requested analysis deliberately focuses on false target assertions; it therefore does not cover every form of deception.

The strongest feasible design measures knowledge independently, manipulates incentives on matched questions, and preserves unresolved cases. Correct neutral answers support an operational known-answer label; they do not prove that the belief remains available under pressure. A false response from an uncertain model supports epistemic-failure attribution but does not prove absence of latent knowledge. Answer changes, self-confessions and detector scores must not serve as their own ground truth. “Hallucination” also varies by paper: semantic-entropy work emphasizes arbitrary uncertain errors, whereas confident misconceptions remain epistemic failures under this project’s broader definition.

## Key papers

**Ren et al. (2025), The MASK Benchmark: Disentangling Honesty From Accuracy in AI Systems.** [arXiv2503.03750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03750). Neutral repeated belief elicitation is compared with pressured statements and ground truth across six archetypes. The paper evaluates 1,500 examples, including private data; our pinned public download contains 1,000. It reports 86.4% human/pipeline agreement on 150 examples and finds accuracy scaling need not imply honesty scaling. Prompting and LoRRA are mitigation comparators, not mechanism classifiers. Honesty=1−P(lie) includes no-belief/evasive cases, so it cannot answer the requested false-output composition. Code: [centerforaisafety/mask](https://github.com/centerforaisafety/mask). Reuse prompts and parsing; independently regenerate model-specific labels.

**Goldowsky-Dill, Chughtai, Heimersheim and Hobbhahn (2025), Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes.** [arXiv2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407). Llama3.3-70B residual activations at layer 22 train a standardized L2 logistic probe from contrastive instructed contexts. Evaluation covers roleplay, insider reports/confirmations and sandbagging; comparisons include roleplay-trained probes, follow-ups, SAE features and an external judge. Reported AUROC .964–.999 suggests transfer within these settings, but honest pressure-bearing responses and confessions can score highly. Token aggregation, threshold domain and layer matter substantially. [Official code](https://github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection) provides training and evaluation machinery. The later Cooney calibration correction below limits interpretation of original low-FPR recall.

**Kretschmar, Laurito, Maiya and Marks (2025), Liars’ Bench.** [arXiv2511.16035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16035). Seven settings, four model families and 72,863 examples test private/self/world-knowledge transfer. External judge, self-evaluation, unrelated-question classifier and mean activation probe average balanced accuracy .73/.66/.60/.54 respectively under their calibration protocol. A privileged follow-up probe reaches BA .73, AUROC .91. Strong within-setting ranking can coexist with poor fixed-threshold recall. Neutral belief correctness and context persistence are imperfect; some “lies” are factually correct. [Code](https://github.com/Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench) is useful for baseline definitions and held-out-setting evaluation, not a clean ready-made hallucination-versus-lie dataset.

**Natarajan, Jain, Arora, Golechha and Bloom (2026), One Probe Won’t Catch Them All.** [arXiv2602.01425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01425). Gemma2-9B experiments cover16 deception types and a 2,000-probe sweep. Prompt choice accounts for 70.6% of variance within the restricted sweep. Mean AUC .689 baseline rises to .721 with validation-selected universal training and .797 with a post-hoc taxonomy oracle. Only the first improvement corresponds to deployable selection. Synthetic targeted prompts beat baseline in 12/16 types; broad transfer remains unresolved. No official repository was identified in the PDF. Appendix prompt pairs can support a small preregistered sensitivity analysis, not an unrestricted test-set search.

**Thormann (2026), Probing the Limits of the Lie Detector Approach to LLM Deception.** [arXiv2603.10003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10003). A 97-case forced-choice dataset contrasts false lies with misleading truths across three small models. Attention-head truth probes perform much worse on misleading truths in raw format; Llama performance is .835 versus .536, improving to .742 for misleading truths in dialogue. Intent and recipient false beliefs are not established. [Code](https://github.com/Tom-Felix-Thormann/Probing_Limits_Lie_Detectors). This bounds our scope: detecting false assertions is not comprehensive deception detection.

**Luikham (2026), Asymmetries in Spontaneous and Instructed Deception.** [arXiv2609.00180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00180). Llama3.1-70B synthetic fabrication/omission cases compare incentive-elicited and instructed probes. Spontaneous→instructed BA reaches .980; reverse transfer is weaker. Judge agreement on 120 examples is .85 for fabrication/.82 for omission. Different labeling methods across conditions, supplied-fact assumptions and one-model scope limit causal interpretation. Steering also affects perplexity. [Code](https://github.com/JosiahL98/Spont-Instructed-Deception). Retain transfer diagnostics; omit steering from the bounded experiment.

**Moustafa, Feser and Mai (2026), Beyond Liars’ Bench.** [arXiv2607.20479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20479). Seven detector families compare depths and dense/SAE representations using DolusChat training and four Liars’ Bench targets. Mistral24B/Gemma2-27B results show no uniform improvement from depth, sparsity or expressivity; some transfer AUROCs fall below chance. Recall uses a .5 threshold, not a calibrated 1% FPR. [Code](https://github.com/amrgaber249/Beyond-Liars-Bench). Supports simple linear baselines and deliberate domain controls before costly representation searches.

**Cheang, Chan, Zhang and Deng (2025; revised2026), Do LLMs Really Know What They Don’t Know?** [arXiv2510.09033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09033). On 12,293 Wikidata cloze questions, subject-attention interventions separate associated from unassociated errors in Llama3-8B/Mistral7B. Attention/hidden-state/probability/popularity detectors distinguish unassociated errors much better (roughly .86–.93 AUROC) than associated confident errors (.48–.69). Semantic consistency shows a similar gap. Labels depend on the attention intervention, making some attention analyses partly circular. [Code](https://github.com/AndyCheang/knowledge-recall-vs-truthfulness). This motivates retaining stable wrong answers as an epistemic subgroup; it does not justify labeling confident errors as lies.

**Cooney, Africa and Irving (2026), “Did you lie?”** [arXiv2606.12618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12618). Verified TriviaQA under varied prompts tests 31 models; 13 trained organisms receive stronger belief checks. Apollo/unrelated-question median BA falls to .52/.50 on organisms, while a trained follow-up activation detector reaches .65 and CoT judge .82. Reasoning-based selection advantages CoT-visible judges. Appendix E reports pooled-token versus per-response calibration in Apollo: correcting aggregation reduces Roleplaying TPR 96%→73.2%. These are replication authors’ findings, not independently reproduced here. Public artifacts are linked through [the AISI collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/ai-safety-institute/lie-detection). Reuse the belief-verification argument and response-level threshold protocol; omit organism training.

**Kossen, Han, Razzak, Schut, Malik and Gal (2024), Semantic Entropy Probes.** [arXiv2406.15927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15927). Logistic probes predict high semantic entropy from a single generation’s hidden states. Training entropy uses ten sampled answers clustered by bidirectional entailment; inference needs one response. TriviaQA, NQ, SQuAD and BioASQ comparisons include correctness probes, log-likelihood, naive entropy, P(True) and sampled semantic entropy. SEP generally transfers better than correctness probes but trails expensive sampling baselines. High entropy-prediction AUROC is not factuality AUROC. Answer-type shortcuts and confident errors matter. [Official code](https://github.com/OATML/semantic-entropy-probes) supplies both SE and SEP, making it the primary hallucination baseline.

**Hopkins, Khullar, Wang and Roger (2026), Fine-Tuned Lie Detectors Failed to Generalize.** [Anthropic article](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/lie-detectors/). Approximately 200,000 on-policy examples across 12 settings feed LoRA binary transcript classifiers. In-distribution AUROC improves .60→.95; held-out-category performance remains .70–.75. Roughly 25% of initial labels change after judge review; human checks are small. The rubric combines factuality, omission and belief evidence. This strengthens the case for category-held-out tests and audited uncertainty labels, while remaining a text-classifier result rather than a white-box mechanism proof.

## Common methods, baselines and metrics
The literature combines repeated neutral knowledge queries, contrastive instructed activations, logistic probes, follow-up self-assessment, semantic sampling and external judges. Their information access differs: a full-transcript judge can see incentives or private reasoning that a response-only detector does not. Record access explicitly and keep gold answers out of detector inputs. Linear probe coefficients and checkpoints are model-specific.

Recommended baseline set: Apollo-style mean-response probe; a clearly specified trained follow-up probe; SEP; neutral-trained correctness probe; sampled semantic entropy and normalized response log-likelihood. Add prompt-only and answer-text classifiers to expose shortcuts. A supervised L-versus-H classifier is a separate diagnostic, not evidence that a standard frozen detector already distinguishes mechanisms.

Report AUROC and AUPRC for L versus H **among false outputs**, plus each versus truthful outputs. Include balanced accuracy, confusion matrices, macro-F1, response-level TPR at 1%/5% FPR and realized subgroup FPR. Use question-cluster bootstrap intervals. Report false-output shares on the original sampled arm distribution, never on a class-balanced detector set. Dataset papers’ scores are not directly comparable because labels, models, access and thresholds differ.

## Experiment recommendations and remaining gap
Use deduplicated TriviaQA no-context train/validation for matched questions and MASK known_facts for external validity; provided_facts is a separate comprehension-controlled intervention. TriviaQA test answers are unavailable. Existing datasets supply questions and prompts, not ground-truth epistemic mechanisms for the chosen model.

[planning.md](planning.md) scores eight directions and retains exactly three: operational mechanism stratification, detector specificity, and bounded transfer/measurement robustness. Freeze one model and independent knowledge checks; keep honest pressured, confident wrong neutral, and unresolved responses. Split question/entity groups before any variants. Select layers and thresholds only on validation data and calibrate after response aggregation. Use independent samples for knowledge labels and entropy features to avoid circular separation.

The unresolved scientific question is whether matched false answers carry detector signals beyond uncertainty, topic and incentive framing. This resource phase supplies no measured lie/hallucination percentage. Any eventual shares are conditional on the chosen model, question distribution, incentive mixture and operational labeling rules; they do not identify deployment-wide prevalence or latent intent.
