We conduct a bounded diagnostic replication on QWEN2.5-7B-INSTRUCT, explicitly a historical
baseline rather than a current frontier model. We sample 600 unique TRIVIAQA questions, split
canonical answer entities before fitting detectors, and collect independent knowledge samples, five
matched response conditions, and a private factual follow-up after every response (figure 1). Our
labels retain uncertain neutral errors, repetitions of stable wrong beliefs, recovered incentive mis-
reports, and unresolved false outputs. We use the term recovered misreport proxy because neither
neutral consistency nor subsequent recovery proves intent.
The behavioral yield is the central constraint: only two incentive responses satisfy the recovery cri-
terion, and only one reaches the test split. In the four-arm mixture, 60.49% of strictly scored false
outputs remain unresolved. The clearest detector result concerns context. An instructed-deception
response probe achieves AUROC 0.972 against uncertain neutral errors, while its prompt-only coun-
terpart achieves 1.000. At a threshold calibrated on neutral correct answers, the response probe flags
12/53 known-correct reward answers and 2/54 known-correct neutral answers. We therefore report
a specificity diagnostic and a feasibility limit, not a reliable estimate of genuine-lie detection.
Our contributions are:
• We construct a matched-question protocol with independent knowledge and uncertainty samples,
universal follow-ups, and a conservative four-way partition of false outputs.
• We measure prompt sensitivity through false-versus-false contrasts and correct pressured controls,
showing that high instructed-condition AUROC coexists with perfect prompt-only separation.
• We quantify the low recovered-case yield and assess label, threshold, and external-dataset sensi-
tivity without replacing the underpowered primary comparison with instructed controls.
