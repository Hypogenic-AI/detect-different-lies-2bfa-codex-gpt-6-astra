\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\para{Honesty, factuality, and belief evidence.}
\maskbench compares elicited beliefs, pressured statements, and reference answers to separate honesty from accuracy~\cite{ren2025mask}. Cooney et al. add belief-verified evaluations and find substantial detector failures across models and trained organisms~\cite{cooney2026did}. We adopt their measurement concern, rather than claim a new honesty taxonomy. Our narrower contribution combines matched factual questions, independent knowledge strata, false-versus-false evaluation, and prompt controls in one pipeline. Cheang et al. distinguish knowledge recall from truthfulness and show that confident errors challenge internal-state detectors~\cite{cheang2025know}; this motivates retaining stable wrong beliefs instead of merging them with uncertainty or misreporting.

\para{Activation probes and transfer.}
Goldowsky-Dill et al. train linear probes on instructed contrasts and report transfer to strategic-deception settings, while also observing high scores for some honest deception-related responses~\cite{goldowskydill2025detecting}. \emph{Liars' Bench} evaluates transfer across lie settings~\cite{kretschmar2025liars}. Targeted-probe experiments emphasize training-prompt sensitivity~\cite{natarajan2026one}; comparisons of spontaneous and instructed deception motivate keeping the two conditions distinct~\cite{luikham2026asymmetries}. Broader architectural comparisons find no uniform benefit from greater depth, sparsity, or expressivity~\cite{moustafa2026beyond}. We consequently start with model-specific linear adaptations and simple controls. We do not reuse published checkpoints or equate our fictional quiz incentives with verified strategic behavior.

\para{Uncertainty, calibration, and scope.}
Semantic entropy probes predict uncertainty from hidden states using entailment-clustered answer samples~\cite{kossen2024semantic}. Our three-sample normalized-answer entropy is a cheaper approximation, with different clustering and token aggregation, so our findings do not evaluate full \sep. Hopkins et al. document generalization and label-audit difficulties for fine-tuned transcript classifiers~\cite{hopkins2026fine}; their model access and training differ from our activation probes, but their results motivate held-out conditions and explicit auditing. Finally, deception can involve omissions and misleading truths~\cite{thormann2026probing}. Our denominator contains false target answers only and cannot support conclusions about those broader behaviors.
