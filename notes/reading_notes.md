# Literature review: Detect different lies

Review in progress. Scope: distinguish epistemic failure from incentive-driven misreporting in one fixed open-weight model on matched questions. Include belief verification, white-box deception probes, uncertainty/hallucination detectors, and transfer tests (2023–2026 plus foundational work). Exclude unrelated applications and purely factual benchmarks as evidence of lying.

Search terms: belief-verified deception; honesty versus accuracy; linear deception probes; semantic entropy probes; knowledge recall versus truthfulness; spontaneous versus instructed deception; matched-question hallucination; detector cross-domain transfer.

## Sequential reading notes

- **2503.03750 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** MASK operationalizes belief through consistency under neutral queries; lying compares statement to belief, accuracy compares belief to ground truth. Six adversarial archetypes and realistic misleading audiences; paper reports 1,500 evaluated examples including a private 500. Avoid evidence-changing prompts, subjective facts, and trivial roleplay. Intent is built into scenarios rather than independently verified. Official code: centerforaisafety/mask.
- **2503.03750 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Extract proposition resolutions then compare programmatically; three direct elicitation samples plus indirect binary consistency checks. Human/pipeline agreement 86.4% on 150 examples. No-belief and evasive cases are tracked separately; honesty 1−P(lie) includes these cases, so it is not the desired share among false statements. Prompting/LoRRA improve honesty but do not eliminate lying; references cover uncertainty and representation baselines.
- **2503.03750 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** Appendix emphasizes English, judge noise and adversarial selection limitations. Neutral consistency is a behavioral proxy for belief. Self-reported lying agrees 83.6% with pipeline on selected clear responses; not independent proof. Human pressure prompts, automated auxiliary labels, and manual audits underpin construction.
- **2503.03750 chunk 4 (pp. 19–22):** Provided-facts archetype has no neutral belief elicitation; do not assume private facts were understood. Lying@10 is any lie in ten attempts, distinct from per-response prevalence. Belief-normalized scaling correlations differ materially; model comparison is not needed for this project. Full results include Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B as feasible candidates.
- **2511.16035 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** 72,863 on-policy examples across seven settings/four model families; assertion-based lying does not require measured intent. Neutral belief checks may accept consistently wrong beliefs. World/self knowledge and context/inherent reasons are separate axes. MASK excluded because single-proposition labels miss other falsehoods. Some fine-tuned organism labels remain inferential.
- **2511.16035 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Baselines: external judge, self-evaluation, 39-question logprob classifier, mean activation logistic probe (20th-percentile layer, regularization λ=10) and follow-up variants. Calibrate at ≤1% Alpaca FPR; average balanced accuracy 0.73/0.66/0.60/0.54 respectively. Privileged follow-up probe BA .73, AUROC .91. High AUROC can coexist with near-zero recall under control-domain threshold shift. Dataset-specific optimization prohibited for benchmark baseline; our within-task diagnostic must be labeled separately.
- **2511.16035 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** References, deployment/audit use cases and formal caveat: consistent neutral belief need not be salient during reporting. Detector taxonomy separates access, message scope, interactivity and semantic verification.
- **2511.16035 chunk 4 (pp. 19–24):** Harm-pressure generation uses neutral answer/knowledge templates, held-out neutral contradiction checks and ≥4× contradiction filtering. This supports adding a neutral repeat control, not treating every answer flip as intentional.
- **2511.16035 chunk 5 (pp. 25–30):** Incorrect beliefs can yield factually correct lies; reported p(incorrect belief|lying)=.33 and p(correct answer|lying)=.15 in HP-C. Our false-output-only estimand intentionally omits these true lies. Appendices enumerate pressure template variants.
- **2511.16035 chunk 6 (pp. 31–36):** Remaining harm/knowledge-report templates separate denial of knowledge from refusal; invalid responses discarded in original benchmark. Gender-secret training combines implicit attribute answers with concealment, motivating concern that learned response patterns may substitute for beliefs.
- **2511.16035 chunk 7 (pp. 37–42):** Attribute-awareness tests improve after fine-tuning but are imperfect; paired two-turn checks strengthen labels. Convincing-game accepts only stable contrasting properties. Soft-trigger baseline learns greeting/time/name categories; trigger detection can masquerade as lie detection.
- **2511.16035 chunk 8 (pp. 43–48):** Instructed truth/lie templates and insider-trading generation documented. Insider labels have estimated 5–25% interpretive ambiguity. External judge errors include moral approval of protective lying, mistaken model-knowledge assumptions and factual ignorance.
- **2511.16035 chunk 9 (pp. 49–54):** Self-evaluation prompts and unrelated-question classifier implementation; 80 training questions and 39 binary follow-ups. Additional tables make control-domain threshold failures visible. Keep probability/logit versus sampled binary implementations distinct.
- **2511.16035 chunk 10 (pp. 55–60):** Tables 7–12 report model/dataset-specific AUROC, BA, recall and realized target-domain FPR. Follow-up probe average BA .58 versus mean .54; privileged probe stronger. Target FPR often far exceeds 1% despite calibration, so report realized FPR by group.
- **2511.16035 chunk 11 (pp. 61–66):** Alternative judge/self-judge tables and score distributions confirm strong domain/model dependence; changing lie to deception wording does not solve private-knowledge detection.
- **2511.16035 chunk 12 (pp. 67–72):** Default system prompts, probe training pair and Apollo replication. Layer22 versus layer20 and inclusion/exclusion of scratchpad materially alter recall. MASK examples contain unsupported extra claims despite correct pivotal proposition; scope our unit to the target proposition and audit extraneous claims separately.
- **2511.16035 chunk 13 (pp. 73–78):** Complete HP-C/HP-KR examples illustrate short choice answers and knowledge denials; identical surface denial can have different labels depending on separately established knowledge.
- **2511.16035 chunk 14 (pp. 79–84):** Gender-secret, convincing-game and soft-trigger transcripts illustrate private knowledge, persona assertions and trigger-conditioned answers. Surface cues differ substantially across settings.
- **2511.16035 chunk 15 (pp. 85–90):** Remaining soft-trigger examples pair passage comprehension with unrelated name/greeting/time cues. Keep source passages/questions grouped in splits to prevent leakage.
- **2511.16035 chunk 16 (pp. 91–96):** Instructed-deception examples and full insider-trading setup show why explicit instruction labels and naturally generated report labels should remain separate.
- **2511.16035 chunk 17 (pp. 97–102):** Insider-trading transcripts expose private reasoning/actions before public reporting; final-answer-only versus reasoning-inclusive feature extraction is a substantive experimental choice.
- **2511.16035 chunk 18 (pp. 103–108):** Additional Llama/Mistral insider examples contrast explicit disclosure with plausible-deniability concealment. Long trajectories complicate response-level labels and mean pooling.
- **2511.16035 chunk 19 (pp. 109–112):** Final insider denial and Alpaca examples complete the appendix. Alpaca benign instruction labels are assumed non-lying, not verified correctness; uncertainty false positives on benign data need separate measurement.
- **2502.03407 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Apollo standardized residual-stream logistic probe λ=10 at layer22/80 of Llama3.3-70B; instructed pairs use identical teacher-forced true prefixes excluding last five tokens. Evaluation on-policy, mean response-token scores. AUROC .964–.999 across four settings; control-calibrated recall 95–99% does not imply low FPR on honest incentive-bearing cases.
- **2502.03407 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Confessions and deception-related contexts produce false positives; token averaging can dilute short lies. Comparators include roleplay training, SAE features, follow-up probe, single-question direction and GPT-4o judge. Labels partly judge-derived; ambiguous examples excluded.
- **2502.03407 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** Paired roleplay control confirms instructed-pair probe also tracks incentive context. AI-audit case rejected because errors could be confusion. Follow-up probes sometimes improve low-FPR detection; absent causal grounding, separability alone does not establish mechanism.
- **2502.03407 chunk 4 (pp. 19–24):** Ablations cover difference-of-means, LDA, LAT, regularization, layer, max/ReLU/mean pooling and final token. Layer changes can collapse fixed-threshold recall. Reclassifying ambiguous examples reduces performance; include sensitivity analysis instead of silently dropping them.
- **2502.03407 chunk 5 (pp. 25–30):** Token heatmaps and complete grader rubrics distinguish explicit disclosure, omission and ambiguity. GPT-generated roleplay scenarios are vetted but remain synthetic. Insider trajectories are selected on successful actions, not population samples.
- **2502.03407 chunk 6 (pp. 31–35):** Full trading/sandbagging prompts and judge criteria. White-box scoring excludes private reasoning in main evaluations; black-box judge sees fuller transcript. Preserve matched information access when comparing methods.
- **2602.01425 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Gemma2-9B main model; 2,000-probe sweep attributes 70.6% of variance to prompt choice (restricted layer sweep). Mean AUC .689 baseline, .721 validation-selected universal, .797 post-hoc taxonomy oracle. Oracle is not deployable selection. Figure/text differ on some dataset-specific values; use aggregate figure and avoid claiming a single exact MASK improvement. Synthetic matched probes outperform baseline in 12/16 types.
- **2602.01425 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Probe-score clusters and example heatmaps suggest semantic heterogeneity; intent interpretation remains a hypothesis, not a causal identification. Dataset overview spans MASK, Liars’ Bench and Apollo. Only ANOVA replicated on Llama70B; primary full comparison is Gemma9B.
- **2602.01425 chunk 3 (pp. 13–16):** Complete taxonomy prompt pairs, framing controls and 11 synthetic factual categories support reproducible prompt sensitivity checks. Narrow study should preselect variants, not search many prompts on the test labels. No repository link identified in PDF.
- **2603.10003 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Thormann distinguishes non-false misleading utterances from false lies. 97 manually built forced-choice cases; three 7–9B models. Truth probe selects top10 attention heads on validation data; raw versus dialogue formats. Llama raw detects .835 false lies versus .536 misleading truths; dialogue .835/.742. This is an adjacent boundary, not evidence for our epistemic/incentive distinction.
- **2603.10003 chunk 2 (pp. 7–10):** Limitations acknowledge forced-choice task, no measured recipient false beliefs and weak evidence of strategic intent; calls for second-order-belief probes. Code/data: https://github.com/Tom-Felix-Thormann/Probing_Limits_Lie_Detectors. Prune as implementation direction because our estimand is explicitly false statements.
- **2609.00180 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Llama3.1-70B synthetic known-fact scenarios compare instructed versus incentive-elicited fabrication/omission. Response probes transfer asymmetrically (spontaneous→instructed BA .980; reverse best .893); direction cosines ~.5. Instruction labels used for instructed outputs, judge labels for spontaneous outputs: a confound. Detection token position differs from steering optimum.
- **2609.00180 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Single-model limitation and synthetic scenario realism unresolved. Separate balanced consolidated data from unpruned steering evaluation. Ground truth in system prompt does not itself verify belief. Definitions and prompts are in appendix.
- **2609.00180 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** Judge validation n=120, binary fabrication agreement .85 and omission .82; judges sometimes confuse omission with fabrication. Expanded layer/seed transfer grids and steering tables; only three seeds and no epistemic-failure arm.
- **2609.00180 chunk 4 (pp. 19–22):** Random-direction controls and WikiText perplexity expose degradation at strong steering. Code: https://github.com/JosiahL98/Spont-Instructed-Deception. Steering pruned; transfer is retained as a bounded diagnostic.
- **2607.20479 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Seven probe families, 20%/66% depths, Mistral24B dense and Gemma2-27B dense/SAE comparisons. Train on 1,000 DolusChat examples; four Liars’ Bench targets. No uniform depth/expressivity advantage; some HP-KR AUROCs .12–.16. Recall at .5 is not low-FPR calibrated. Anti-transfer is observed, but interpretation as inverted deception mechanisms is stronger than evidence warrants. Code amrgaber249/Beyond-Liars-Bench.
- **2607.20479 chunk 2 (pp. 7–9):** Typology-specific training varies with task/depth; mixture not always best. Dense/SAE broadly similar, INLP does not uniformly erase signal. Limited layers, default thresholds and uncertain on-policy/model-organism matching constrain conclusions. Prune expensive SAE/nonlinear sweep.
- **2510.09033 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** 12,293 Wikidata cloze questions; Llama3-8B counts FA3506/AH1406/UH7381. AH/UH operationally split by subject-attention masking JS divergence relative to correct-answer mean. This is not an incentive-based taxonomy; confident associated errors must not be relabeled lies. Attention analysis partly inherits label construction; paper acknowledges this.
- **2510.09033 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Subject/attention/last-token probes, token probability and popularity baselines struggle on AH (Llama AUROC ~.48–.69) versus UH (~.86–.93). Refusal tuning UH82% versus AH33%, with factual over-refusal. Mechanism claims bounded to factual cloze tasks and two base models.
- **2510.09033 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** Four relations (father, mother, spouse, birth date); NER then Qwen2.5-14B judge labels factuality, 4×L40S setup. Five-seed intervals and semantic-consistency check show similar AH difficulty. Code AndyCheang/knowledge-recall-vs-truthfulness. Our neutral repeated-answer strata are behavioral proxies, not replicas of attention-intervention AH/UH labels.
- **2606.12618 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Cooney, Africa and Irving distinguish weak prompted-behaviour detection from belief-grounded lying. 31 models and 13 trained organisms; Varied Deception uses verified TriviaQA and correct-neutral filtering. Context-preserving belief evidence remains essential; roleplay can change beliefs.
- **2606.12618 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** Apollo/unrelated-question median BA .52/.50 on organisms versus DYL .65 and CoT judge .82. DYL trains follow-up answer activations on confessing and doubling-down examples, unlike a raw self-report baseline. CoT-grounded selection advantages CoT-visible judges; report access and selection bias. HF release: ai-safety-institute/lie-detection collection.
- **2606.12618 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** References and structured belief-assurance argument separate behavioural, internal and instillation evidence. Correctness in another context alone cannot establish knowledge during the false report. No experiment here identifies deployment-wide prevalence.
- **2606.12618 chunk 4 (pp. 19–24):** Appendix E reports original Apollo implementation pools calibration tokens rather than aggregating each response first; corrected Roleplaying TPR drops 96% to 73.2%. This is a reported replication finding, not independently reproduced here. Targeted Apollo median BA .81 vs .84 standard; richer training is not uniformly better. WMDP audit flags ground-truth uncertainty, supporting benign factual questions and manual audits.
- **2606.12618 chunk 5 (pp. 25–30):** False confessions differ by model; self-report cannot ground labels alone. Prompted Gender Secret also defeats Apollo, so organism failure is not entirely fine-tuning shift. Complete organism prompts distinguish fabrication, concealment and biased framing; only false target assertions belong in our primary estimand.
- **2606.12618 chunk 6 (pp. 31–32):** Final system prompt and full judge rubric distinguish false beliefs, evasion and statements contradicting belief. CoT and contextual knowledge are judge inputs, so comparisons with answer-only probes require explicit access labels.
- **2406.15927 chunk 1 (pp. 1–6):** Kossen et al. train logistic SE probes on entropy of ten semantic-clustered samples, at last input or final response token. Four QA tasks (TriviaQA, NQ, SQuAD, BioASQ), five models; compare accuracy probe, log-likelihood, naive entropy, P(True), sampled SE. SE-label AUROC .7–.95 is not factuality AUROC.
- **2406.15927 chunk 2 (pp. 7–12):** SEP transfers better than accuracy probes in tested task shifts, though sampled SE remains stronger. Yes/no structure in BioASQ can give a shortcut; use matched answer formats. Confident errors and incentive-driven consistent lies need not have high SE. Main-text descriptions of leave-one-out training differ; use explicit Appendix B.5 protocol when replicating.
- **2406.15927 chunk 3 (pp. 13–18):** References and layer/position result grids; accurate SE prediction does not establish accurate detection of all falsehoods. Residual/MLP alternatives considered in appendix; pretrained checkpoints are model-specific.
- **2406.15927 chunk 4 (pp. 19–22):** N=10, T=1, top-p=.9, top-k=50; short-form DeBERTa entailment, long-form GPT3.5. Training2000 short/1000 long across tasks; long-form entropy filtering sometimes used. Appendix B.5 trains each dataset then tests others for aggregated results. Do not choose layers using test labels; our single-model adaptation must be described as adaptation. Code OATML/semantic-entropy-probes.
- **Anthropic web article (all sections including appendices):** Hopkins, Khullar, Wang, Roger (Aug21 2026), ~200k examples/12settings. Gemma LoRA rank16/alpha32/dropout.05, five epochs, balanced category halves; in-distribution AUROC .60→.95 versus held-out .70–.75. GPT5 relabeling changes ~25%; human judge checks only5–10/category.
